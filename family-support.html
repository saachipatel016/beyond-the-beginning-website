For parents and caregivers

You do not have to know every answer today.

A few practical prompts and reminders to help you navigate conversations, ask for support, and take things one step at a time.

Conversation prompts

Questions you can ask your care team.

  • What are the main goals for my baby today?
  • Can you explain this equipment or term in everyday language?
  • What changes should I tell the team about right away?
  • Who can I speak with if I need emotional or practical support?
  • What should I understand before the next step in care?

These are conversation starters, not a checklist for making care decisions.

Care for yourself

Your needs matter, too.

It can be hard to absorb information when you are worried or exhausted. If it helps, ask someone you trust to take notes, request an interpreter, or ask the care team to explain something again.

You can also ask whether a hospital social worker, family-support specialist, lactation consultant, or mental-health professional is available.

Preparing for a transition

Make space for questions.

Every baby's plan is different. Ask your clinical team to explain any follow-up visits, feeding plans, medications, equipment, warning signs, and who to contact after leaving the hospital.

Only your baby's qualified care team can advise you about readiness for discharge.

People around you

Let others help in specific ways.

If you want support, consider asking a friend or family member to bring a meal, help with transportation, care for siblings, or handle a small task at home.

It is okay to tell people what would actually be helpful—and it is okay to say no when you need quiet.
